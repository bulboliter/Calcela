What is Simple Interest? (Definition & Formula)

Simple interest is interest calculated only on the principal amount of a loan or investment. Unlike compound interest, simple interest doesn't accumulate over time, making it ideal for short-term financial products like personal loans, car loans, and certain savings accounts.

Simple Interest Formula Explained

Simple Interest Formula:

Simple Interest Calculator Example:

  • • Principal: $10,000
  • • Annual Interest Rate: 5%
  • • Time Period: 3 years
  • • Simple Interest Earned: $10,000 × 0.05 × 3 = $1,500
  • • Total Future Value: $11,500

Perfect for: Short-term loans, basic savings calculations, financial planning beginners

What is Compound Interest? (The Power of Compounding)

Compound interest is interest calculated on both the original principal and previously earned interest. This creates an exponential growth effect that Albert Einstein allegedly called "the eighth wonder of the world." Most investment accounts, retirement funds, and high-yield savings accounts use compound interest.

Compound Interest Formula

Compound Interest Formula:

Compound Interest Calculator Example:

  • • Principal: $10,000
  • • Annual Interest Rate: 5%
  • • Time Period: 3 years
  • • Compounding: Annually
  • • Total Future Value: $10,000 × (1.05)³ = $11,576.25
  • • Compound Interest Earned: $1,576.25
  • • Extra earnings vs Simple Interest: $76.25

Ideal for: Long-term investments, retirement planning, wealth building strategies

Advanced Interest Calculation Concepts

Compounding Frequency Impact

The frequency of compounding significantly affects your returns:

  • Daily Compounding: 365 times per year - highest returns
  • Monthly Compounding: 12 times per year - common for savings accounts
  • Quarterly Compounding: 4 times per year - typical for CDs
  • Annual Compounding: Once per year - basic compound interest

Interest Calculator FAQ

How accurate is this compound interest calculator?
Our calculator uses industry-standard financial formulas and provides highly accurate results for financial planning. Real-world returns may vary due to market conditions, fees, and account-specific terms.
How often should I compound my investments?
More frequent compounding generally produces higher returns. However, the difference between daily and monthly compounding is often minimal compared to other factors like interest rate and time.
Is simple or compound interest better for borrowers?
Simple interest is better for borrowers because you pay less total interest. Compound interest is better for savers and investors because you earn more money over time.
How do I calculate interest on a savings account?
Most savings accounts use compound interest calculated daily or monthly. Use our compound interest calculator with your account's APY (Annual Percentage Yield) for accurate projections.

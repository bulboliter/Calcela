What is Simple Interest?

Simple interest is calculated only on the principal amount of a loan or investment. It's straightforward to calculate and commonly used for short-term loans, car loans, and some personal loans. The formula remains constant throughout the investment period.

Simple Interest Formula:
Interest = Principal × Rate × Time
Total Amount = Principal + Interest

Example: Simple Interest Calculation

If you invest $10,000 at 5% annual interest for 3 years:
Interest = $10,000 × 0.05 × 3 = $1,500
Total Amount = $10,000 + $1,500 = $11,500

What is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is calculated on both the principal amount and the accumulated interest from previous periods. This creates a snowball effect where your money grows exponentially over time. It's commonly used in savings accounts, investment portfolios, and long-term financial planning.

Compound Interest Formula:
Total Amount = Principal × (1 + Rate)^Time
Interest = Total Amount - Principal

Example: Compound Interest Calculation

Using the same $10,000 at 5% annual interest for 3 years:
Total Amount = $10,000 × (1.05)³ = $11,576.25
Interest = $11,576.25 - $10,000 = $1,576.25

Frequently Asked Questions

How to calculate simple interest on a loan?
To calculate simple interest on a loan, multiply the principal amount by the interest rate and the time period. For example, a $5,000 loan at 6% annual interest for 2 years would generate $600 in interest ($5,000 × 0.06 × 2).
How to calculate compound interest on investments?
Compound interest is calculated using the formula: A = P(1 + r)^t, where A is the final amount, P is the principal, r is the interest rate, and t is time. Our calculator handles these complex calculations automatically.
What's the difference between simple and compound interest?
Simple interest is calculated only on the principal amount, while compound interest is calculated on both the principal and accumulated interest. Compound interest results in higher returns over time due to the compounding effect.
Can I use this calculator for monthly contributions?
This calculator focuses on lump-sum investments. For calculations with monthly contributions, you would need an interest calculator with monthly contributions feature, which accounts for regular deposits.
How accurate is this interest calculator?
